# EMBERNET: real-data perception handoff

Date: 2026-10-31. Written at the user's request after a side conversation.
Read alongside README.md, STATUS.md and TRAINING.md. This records a correction
to the scientific direction and a proposed implementation sequence; it does not
claim that new data, models or integrations have been implemented.

## User's concern and direction

The user challenged the value of training perception on our simple procedural
images: "it's trained on synthetic data that's the problem". They want useful
visual intelligence grounded in real aerial observations. They endorsed FLAME 3
("flame 3 is good") and asked for the approach that makes the most sense.

The issue is the training distribution and evaluation, not merely model size or
the appearance of the 3D presentation. Better-looking scenery alone does not
change the images actually entering the perception model.

The user endorsed the broad direction. Specific architecture choices, planner
design and milestones below are recommendations discussed in the conversation,
not individually approved or completed implementation decisions.

## What the existing system actually proves

- The custom dual-encoder RGB/thermal CNN has 70,908 parameters (checked from
  build_network). It is already small, but trained from scratch on stylized
  64x64 procedural images. V2 used 2,048 generated training examples.
- Training and validation use the same procedural renderer. High segmentation
  scores show performance in that synthetic domain, not real-camera capability.
- Current severity targets and thermal values are generated by the simulator.
  They are not calibrated measurements of real fire severity.
- The 4,320 initial mission demonstrations came from our handwritten controller,
  not human pilots or firefighters. PPO subsequently practised in simulation.
- The learned mission actor proposes tasks/bids. Negotiation, release and flight
  safety are explicit logic. Preserve that separation.

Retain old checkpoints, reports and logs as prototype evidence. Describe their
scope accurately rather than deleting results or retroactively upgrading claims.

## Recommended perception and data approach

1. **Audit FLAME 3 first.** Inventory accessible RGB, radiometric TIFFs, timestamps,
   sequences, metadata and actual annotations. Preserve originals and build a
   source manifest with burn/flight IDs, pairings, units, provenance and hashes.
   Verify licensing, access and contents before committing to a training task.
2. **Split by burn where possible.** Keep complete burns out of training; otherwise
   hold out complete flights/sequences and state the weaker generalization claim.
   Adjacent video frames must not leak across splits. If only one burn is
   available, do not call within-burn testing cross-burn validation.
3. **Establish a thermal baseline.** Use radiometric measurements directly for hot
   region candidates and thermal statistics. Compare learning against this simple
   approach rather than teaching a network to reproduce available measurements.
4. **Start with a pretrained RGB segmenter.** MobileNetV3 + LR-ASPP, approximately
   3.2M parameters in its standard pretrained form, is a candidate to benchmark.
   Fine-tune for fire regions; add smoke only with appropriate labels. Preserve
   enough resolution for small targets and measure latency on target hardware.
5. **Add multimodal fusion only when justified.** Initially process RGB and
   thermal separately and associate regions with alignment uncertainty. Compare
   thermal-only, RGB-only and combined performance before choosing complexity.

FLAME 3's paper reports residual RGB/thermal offsets up to roughly 20 pixels.
Synchronization does not imply pixel alignment. Validate registration before
dense fusion. Preserve sensor temperature information; a colorized thermal JPEG
is a visualization, not a substitute for the radiometric TIFF.

Some dataset labels use temperature heuristics. Labels derived from thresholds
can bootstrap training, but testing against the same threshold does not establish
independent fire recognition. Build a manually reviewed held-out subset, retain
ambiguous/unknown regions and report label provenance. Evaluate missed small
fires, false alarms, detection delay, segmentation quality and calibration.

## Evidence, severity and incident memory

Replace the opaque learned severity scalar with inspectable evidence: fire region,
confidence, ground extent when geometry supports it, thermal distribution,
registered change over time, visibility, observation time and uncertainty.

Ground area requires verified camera geometry, pose and terrain information.
Apparent growth must be separated from camera motion. Temperature estimates
remain sensor measurements with assumptions, not complete incident severity.
FLAME 3's prescribed burns also do not cover every uncontrolled wildfire regime.

FLAME 3 alone supplies neither suppression outcomes nor a validated conversion
from imagery to required litres. Keep current water-demand formulas explicitly
synthetic until supported by separate data/models. Person detection needs a
separate real-data evaluation; HIT-UAV was discussed as a thermal starting point,
but its scenes do not validate forest rescue. A person detection does not establish
injury, survivorship or successful rescue.

Each drone's incident record should retain source observations, original evidence
timestamps, uncertainty, status, deferral reason, review deadline and versioned
acknowledged commitments. Aging evidence increases uncertainty; revisit it for
confirmation. Deferred does not mean cleared or forgotten. Actors receive only
own state/observations and delivered messages.

## Coordination and mission learning

Strengthen the understandable baseline before further mission training. A planner
should repeatedly compare feasible assignments using travel/service/recovery time,
resources, uncertainty and existing obligations. Treat possible human exposure as
an urgent verification/response concern rather than relying on fire size alone.

For the large-fire/small-fire example: record the smaller incident, compare which
drone can respond, and request release only if the original response remains
sufficient within the modeled response window. Count fresh acknowledged capacity
and match commitment versions. Keep the original obligation while awaiting release;
do not manufacture permission during a mesh partition. Existing fixed margins and
water estimates are scenario rules, not validated emergency-response standards.

Generate new demonstrations from the improved planner using each actor's available
information. Train RL with observation errors, localization uncertainty, delays
and misses informed by real perception tests, plus resource shortages and mesh
faults. Keep a held-out planner comparison. Learned task/bid preferences must earn
their place through better outcomes; commitment protocol and flight safety remain
independently enforced. Real sensor data improves perception evidence; simulated
RL still provides simulation evidence for coordination and suppression assumptions.

## Demo and implementation order

Maintain two honest evaluation paths:

- **Real-footage replay:** unseen recordings through perception, memory and
  prioritization, showing source images, detections, uncertainty and decisions.
- **Interactive simulation:** actions affect simulated incidents and resources,
  exercising diversion, replenishment, fault handling and learned coordination.

Recorded footage cannot change when a simulated drone chooses another viewpoint
or discharges water. Any mapping of recorded observations into the simulated world
must be explicit, preserve timing/provenance and avoid hidden truth inputs.

Suggested order: data audit and splits -> independent labels and thermal baseline
-> small pretrained RGB model and modality comparison -> incident timeline/replay
-> stronger coordination baseline -> measured perception errors in simulation ->
new demonstrations/RL and held-out comparison. First deliverable: real-data
perception benchmark plus an inspectable incident timeline.

No FLAME 3 files were downloaded, no new training was launched, and no runtime
source was changed in this side conversation. This handoff is the only file added.
Any future Modal jobs remain subject to the main task's verified free-quota bounds.
